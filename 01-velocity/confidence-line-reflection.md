# Confidence Line Reflection (optional)

> Module 1 · Velocity. Toy vs tool, and where this build moved you on the Confidence Line.

Reflection after both Module 1 labs and peer swaps.

## Toy or tool?

The build is still an early prototype. In Lab 1, a peer understood the connection between insight and action but could not judge whether the action was appropriate. In Lab 2, I added a working trend panel and time filter, then separated the observed signal, unknown cause, and proposed experiment. The second peer still questioned why a starter-template nudge was the right response.

The prototype generated a useful learning: inspectable numbers and clearer wording do not supply the missing diagnosis. It can demonstrate an interaction and support discussion, but it is not yet a credible decision tool for choosing an intervention.

## Confidence Line placement

I remain at the high-ambiguity end of the Confidence Line. I can build and refine a functioning interface quickly, but I have not validated the cause of the activation decline or the value of the proposed intervention. Both rounds exposed the same gap at greater depth: the first questioned the connection between metric and action; the second asked for history, alternative explanations, and related metrics.

The next step is to compare audience mix, funnel progression, product errors, and template history before prioritizing an intervention. These are investigations to perform, not findings already established by the sample data.

## The feedback that landed hardest

Round 1: “are the things really related? or just presented together”

Round 2: “What changed 8 weeks ago that makes this the answer?”

I narrowed too quickly to a template nudge. The peer raised plausible alternatives, including changed marketing targeting, a different audience mix, and something broken in the product. I need to support diagnosis and compare hypotheses before promoting one response. I would show the current build as a learning prototype, with its uncertainty explicit, rather than as proof that the recommendation will work.
